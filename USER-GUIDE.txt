===============================================================
 TMAI CONTRACT BUILDER - USER GUIDE
 Build 4
===============================================================

This makes finished contracts from templates. The wording that
never changes lives in the template, written once and checked
once. Each time, you fill in only what changes: the client,
the dates, the fees, which optional clauses apply. The app
writes a new Word document with all of it in place.

The template itself is never changed.

There are two audiences for this guide. People making
contracts want the first half. Whoever looks after the
templates wants the section headed WRITING A TEMPLATE.


---------------------------------------------------------------
 GETTING STARTED
---------------------------------------------------------------

The app is a single file. Nothing needs installing. Copy it
where you like and double-click it.

The first time it runs on a machine, the system will object,
because the app is not signed:

  Windows   "Windows protected your PC". Click More info,
            then Run anyway.

  macOS     It simply refuses. Right-click (or Control-click)
            the app, choose Open, then confirm. After that it
            opens normally.

Then tell it where the templates are: File > Choose templates
folder. A shared folder that everyone uses is ideal, so a
corrected template reaches everyone at once. The folder is
remembered.


---------------------------------------------------------------
 MAKING A CONTRACT
---------------------------------------------------------------

1. Pick the template from the list at the top. Beneath it,
   the field set says what kind of contract it is, normally
   "Publishing agreement". The app picks it by itself; see
   FIELD SETS below.

2. Fill in the form. It asks for exactly what the template
   needs, grouped the way a publishing contract is: Parties,
   Works, Edition, Rights and territory, Term and dates,
   Copies and delivery, Fees and funding.

   Under dates, amounts and numbers, a green line shows how
   it will read in the contract:

     10/5/2026   ->  5th day of October, 2026
     12500       ->  Twelve Thousand Five Hundred Dollars
                     ($12,500.00)

   A red line means it cannot be read. Fix it before going on.

   Dates can be typed most ways: 10/5/2026, Oct 5 2026,
   October 5, 2026. Month comes first, as usual in the US.
   The Today button fills in today's date.

   Amounts can be typed with or without $ and commas.

3. Under "Optional sections", tick the clauses that apply.

4. Press Create contract and choose where to save it.

Before the contract is made, the app warns about two things
and asks whether to go on:

  - anything a publishing agreement is expected to have that
    is empty or marked not relevant, such as a territory or
    the rights licensed. These fields have a * beside them.
  - anything else left empty. Empty things appear in the
    contract as a line, __________, to be filled in by hand.


PARTIES
-------

The form starts with two parties. Party 1 starts as
whoever was Party 1 last time: name, description, address
and signatory. At first that is The Master’s Academy
International, so normally only Party 2 needs filling in.

"+ Add another party" adds a third, or more. "Remove this
party" takes one away again; the first two always stay.


WORKS
-----

The form starts with one work. For a book and its workbook,
press "+ Add another work". The contract then talks about
"the works" instead of "the work" throughout, lists both
titles, and repeats anything that is said for each work,
such as its copyright notice.

A work left completely empty is ignored.


RIGHTS AND TERRITORY
--------------------

Rights licensed is a set of tick boxes: Print, Electronic
(with appropriate digital rights management), Audio book,
Digital, and All formats. Tick any that apply. The green line
shows how the contract will say it, "print and audio book".
All formats covers everything, so the others are greyed out
while it is ticked.

Territory: type the countries or regions, or tick Worldwide.
Worldwide reads "throughout the world" in the contract;
otherwise it reads "in" and what you typed, "in Vietnam".


A FIELD THAT DOES NOT APPLY
---------------------------

Every field has a tick box at its left. Untick it when the
field is not relevant to this contract, say there is no
one-time fee. The box greys out, and in the contract:

  - wording that depends on it is left out, if the template
    puts it inside {{#if ...}} (see WRITING A TEMPLATE)
  - anywhere else, the field is simply left out, with no
    __________ line

Unticking a field marked * is allowed, but the app will
remind you before the contract is made.

Two choices at the bottom of the window are remembered:

  Highlight filled-in wording
      Everything the app filled in is highlighted in yellow,
      so a reviewer can see at a glance what changed from the
      standard wording. Turn it off for the final copy, or
      remove it in Word: select all, then set highlight to
      No Color.

  Open the contract when done
      Opens the finished contract in Word straight away.

Whatever you typed stays in the form when you switch to
another template, so the same details carry across to a
second document.

Clear empties the form and puts back the defaults: two
parties with Party 1 as last time, and one work.


---------------------------------------------------------------
 FIELD SETS
---------------------------------------------------------------

A field set says what a kind of contract should contain. It
decides how the form is laid out, what box each field gets
(tick boxes for rights, Worldwide for territory, a panel per
work and per party), what is filled in to start with, and
which fields are expected.

"Publishing agreement" is built in. Its clusters:

  Parties              repeats; starts with two
  Works                repeats; starts with one
  Edition              language, translator
  Rights and territory rights licensed, territory, exclusive
                       or non-exclusive
  Term and dates       agreement date, term in years,
                       expiration date, publication deadline,
                       signing deadline
  Copies and delivery  print and ebook copy limits, copies to
                       the proprietor, free copies for training
  Fees and funding     one-time fee, funding amount

When a template is chosen, the app picks the field set whose
fields it uses most. To choose another, or none, use the
Field set list. The choice is remembered for that template.

MAKING YOUR OWN

Press "Field sets..." (or Template tools > Field sets).

  New      start an empty one
  Copy     start from the selected one, such as Publishing
           agreement, which cannot itself be changed
  Name it, tick the clusters it should have, and add any
  fields of your own: a name, what kind of box (text, date,
  amount, number, choose one, tick any, territory, yes/no),
  the options for choose one and tick any, whether it is
  expected, and a section heading to group it under.
  Save.

Field sets are saved in a "Field sets" folder inside the
templates folder, so everyone who uses that folder has them.

"Show field codes" lists the {{...}} codes for every field in
the set, ready to copy into a Word template.


---------------------------------------------------------------
 SAVING ANSWERS FOR NEXT TIME
---------------------------------------------------------------

File > Save answers writes what is in the form to a small file.
File > Load answers fills the form from it again.

Use this for renewals and amendments: load last year's
answers, change the dates and fee, and create the new
contract. Answers saved from one template can be loaded into
another; anything that matches by name is filled in.


---------------------------------------------------------------
 MANY CONTRACTS AT ONCE
---------------------------------------------------------------

For a batch, such as the same agreement with twenty clients:

1. Many contracts > Make a blank spreadsheet. It has one
   column for each thing the template asks for. Hover over a
   column heading to see what it expects. Choices and Yes/No
   columns have drop-down lists.

2. Fill in one row per contract. The last column, File Name,
   is optional; left empty, each file is named after the
   template and the client.

3. Many contracts > Create contracts from a spreadsheet.
   Choose the spreadsheet, then the folder for the contracts.

Works and parties get numbered columns: Work 1 Title, Work 2
Title, Party 3 Name and so on, with one spare in case. Leave
the spare ones empty when there is no second work or third
party. Rights licensed takes the options separated by commas,
"Print, Audio book". Territory takes "Worldwide" or the
places. Type N/A in a cell for a field that is not relevant.

A row with a mistake in it, such as a date that cannot be
read, is skipped and the report says why. The others are
still made. A .csv file works as well as an Excel one.


---------------------------------------------------------------
 THE REPORT PANEL
---------------------------------------------------------------

The panel at the bottom says what happened: how many places
were filled in, which optional sections were included or left
out, anything left blank, and where the contract was saved.

When a template is chosen it also says what the template
asks for, and lists any PROBLEM with how it is written.


===============================================================
 WRITING A TEMPLATE
===============================================================

A template is an ordinary Word document, .docx or .dotx. Type
and format it exactly as the finished contract should look.
Then, wherever something changes from one contract to the
next, type a name for it in double braces:

    This Agreement is made between TMAI and {{Client Name}}.

The form will ask for "Client Name" once, and fill it in
everywhere {{Client Name}} appears, including headers,
footers, tables and footnotes. Capitals and spacing in the
name do not matter: {{client name}} is the same field.

The filled-in wording takes the formatting of the name, so
make {{Client Name}} bold to get the client's name in bold.

Help > How to write a template shows a short version of this
section inside the app.


WRITING A TEMPLATE FOR A FIELD SET
----------------------------------

Use the field set's names, and the form, warnings and
defaults all follow. Template tools > Field codes for this
field set lists them. The ones that repeat work like this:

    {{Party 1 Name}}, {{Party 2 Name}}    one party by number
    {{Party Name | list}}                 all of them:
                                          "A and B", "A, B, and C"
    {{Work Title | list}}                 every title
    {{Work Author | list}}                each author once, so
                                          two books by the same
                                          author name him once
    {{Works | plural: work, works}}       follows how many works

For wording that repeats for each work or each party, put
{{#each ...}} and {{/each}} each on a line of their own around
it. Everything between is written out once per work:

    {{#each Works}}
    "This book was first published by {{Work Original
    Publisher}} with the title {{Work Title}}, copyright
    {{Work Copyright}}."
    {{/each}}

Signature blocks are the usual case for parties:

    {{#each Parties}}
    ______________________________   Date ____________
    {{Party Name}}
    {{Party Signatory}}, {{Party Signatory Title}}
    {{/each}}

A third party, if there is one, can be mentioned with an
optional phrase:

    ...and {{Party 2 Name}}{{#if Party 3 Name}}, and
    {{Party 3 Name}}{{/if}}, witnesses:

Rights and territory read naturally inside a sentence:

    ...in {{Rights Licensed}} {{Rights Licensed | plural:
    format, formats}}, {{Territory}} (hereinafter the
    territory)...

gives "in print and audio book formats, throughout the
world". And {{#if Rights Licensed = Audio book}} ... {{/if}}
holds a clause that only applies when audio is licensed.

Where a field might be marked not relevant, keep the words
around it inside an {{#if}} so they go with it:

    ({{#if Party 1 Description}}{{Party 1 Description}},
    {{/if}}hereinafter the proprietor)

The examples folder has "Publishing MOU TEMPLATE.docx",
written this way, as a starting point.

The field set also checks the template: if it has no field
for something expected, such as the territory, Check this
template says so.

The quickest start is usually a contract you already have:
see TURNING A CONTRACT INTO A TEMPLATE below.


FORMATS
-------

Add a bar and a format word to say how a value should read.

    {{Start Date | date}}           October 5, 2026
    {{Start Date | date-formal}}    5th day of October, 2026
    {{Start Date | date-short}}     10/05/2026

    {{Fee | money}}                 $12,500.00
    {{Fee | money-words}}           Twelve Thousand Five
                                    Hundred Dollars ($12,500.00)
    {{Fee | money-whole}}           $12,500  (no cents)

    {{Term | number}}               1,250
    {{Notice Days | number-words}}  thirty (30)

    {{Client Name | upper}}         ACME CORPORATION
    {{Scope | long}}                a large box, for several
                                    lines; each line stays a
                                    line in the contract

    {{Entity | choice: corporation, limited liability company}}
                                    a drop-down list

The format also decides what the form asks for. A date field
only accepts dates, a money field only amounts.

One field can appear in different formats in different
places. It is still asked for once:

    The fee is {{Fee | money-words}} ...
    (and in the fee table)  {{Fee | money}}


OPTIONAL CLAUSES
----------------

Put {{#if ...}} on a line of its own before the clause and
{{/if}} on a line of its own after it:

    {{#if Include Non-Compete}}
    7. Non-Competition. Client shall not ...
    {{/if}}

The form shows "Include Non-Compete" as a tick box. Untick
it and the clause, and the two marker lines, are left out.
Anything can go between the markers: several paragraphs,
headings, whole tables.

Use Word's automatic numbering (Home > Numbering) for clause
numbers, and the clauses after a removed one renumber
themselves. Numbers typed by hand will not.

A cross-reference to a clause number (Insert > Cross-
reference) updates when you select all and press F9 in Word.


ONE BOOK OR SEVERAL
-------------------

An agreement may cover one book, or a book and its workbook.
The wording changes all through it: "the work contains"
becomes "the works contain". Rather than a template for each,
write one that follows the number of titles.

Put the titles in one field, one per line, and show them with
the list format:

    publishing rights to {{Titles | list}} authored by ...

One title gives just the title. Two give "A and B"; three or
more give "A, B, and C". If the field is in italics in the
template, the titles are italic and the "and" between them is
not.

Then, wherever a word depends on how many there are, give the
wording for one and the wording for more:

    The translated {{Titles | plural: work, works}}
    {{Titles | plural: contains, contain}} no matter ...
    shall be {{Titles | plural: an accurate translation,
    accurate translations}}.

When the wording itself has a comma in it, separate the two
with a slash instead:

    {{Titles | plural: it, as written / them, as written}}

plural also follows a number. For "10 years":

    for a period of {{Term}} {{Term | plural: year, years}}

The form shows how many titles it has counted beneath the box.


ALTERNATIVE CLAUSES
-------------------

Where the wording depends on a choice, compare the name with
a value. {{else}} gives the wording for every other case:

    {{#if Governing State = Texas}}
    This Agreement is governed by the laws of Texas, and venue
    lies in Tarrant County.
    {{else}}
    This Agreement is governed by the laws of
    {{Governing State | choice: Texas, Oklahoma, Louisiana}}.
    {{/if}}

A name compared like this, and never printed, becomes a
drop-down of the values it is compared with.

Also allowed:

    {{#if not Include Non-Compete}}    the opposite
    {{#if State != Texas}}             anything but Texas


CLAUSES THAT DEPEND ON A FIELD
------------------------------

A field name alone in an {{#if}} is true when it has been
filled in:

    {{#if Guarantor Name}}
    Guarantee. {{Guarantor Name}} guarantees ...
    {{/if}}

Leave Guarantor Name empty and the guarantee is left out. The
form says so beneath the box.


OPTIONAL WORDS INSIDE A SENTENCE
--------------------------------

A short optional part can sit inside one paragraph. Both
markers must then be in that same paragraph:

    Invoices are due within 30 days{{#if Late Fee}}, and a
    late fee of {{Late Fee | money}} applies{{/if}}.

This works inside table cells too:

    {{#if Late Fee}}{{Late Fee | money}}{{else}}None{{/if}}

If everything in a paragraph is optional and it is left out,
the empty paragraph is removed too.


BLANK LINES BETWEEN CLAUSES
---------------------------

Many contracts separate clauses with an empty line. Leave
those where they are, outside the {{#if}} and {{/if}} lines:

    (empty line)
    {{#if Financial Assistance}}
    11. The publisher can apply for financial assistance ...
    {{/if}}
    (empty line)

When the clause is left out, the two empty lines would end up
together. The app keeps just one, so there is no hole. Empty
lines you typed next to each other on purpose are left alone.


SIGNATURE BLOCKS
----------------

Signature blocks laid out with tabs, two parties side by side,
work, but a long name on the left pushes the right-hand column
along, and a long name on the right wraps back to the margin.
A table with its borders turned off keeps each party in its own
column whatever the length of the name.


CHECKING A TEMPLATE
-------------------

Template tools > Check this template lists every field the
app found, what sort of box it gets, and any problems, such
as:

  - a {{#if}} with no {{/if}}
  - {{Client Name} with a brace missing
  - a format word it does not know
  - an {{#if}} line outside a table whose {{/if}} is inside
    one. Both must be in the same place.

It also lists things WORTH CHECKING. They are not mistakes,
but they would repeat in every contract:

  - tracked changes in the template that were never accepted.
    Filled-in wording inside them would show as a tracked
    change. Review > Accept > Accept All Changes, then save.
  - Track Changes switched on in the template
  - wording formatted as struck through. Strikethrough typed
    as formatting is not a tracked change: it prints, struck
    through, in every contract.
  - a field that is shaded or highlighted, such as an old
    placeholder like NEED YOUR ADDRESS that was shaded grey.
    What is filled in would be shaded too.
  - comments, which are copied into every contract.
  - with a field set chosen, anything expected that the
    template has no field for.
  - a field inside a link. The words shown are filled in,
    but the address the link goes to stays the same.

These are shown in the report as NOTE.

After editing a template in Word, save it and press Reload.


TURNING A CONTRACT INTO A TEMPLATE
----------------------------------

Template tools > Turn a contract into a template.

1. Choose a finished contract you already have.

2. In each row, type the wording that changes exactly as it
   appears in the contract, for example Acme Widgets, Inc.,
   and the field name it should become, Client Name. Pick a
   format if it is a date or an amount.

   "Suggest dates and amounts" finds the dollar amounts and
   dates in the contract and adds them as rows. Give each a
   name, or remove the ones that never change.

3. Press Make template and choose where to save it.

Matching is exact about capitals ("Works" and "works" are
separate rows) and only finds whole words, so "works" is not
found inside "networks".

Curly and straight quotes count as the same, so typing
God's finds the God’s that Word wrote.

The same field can be given to two different pieces of
wording. If a contract calls the other party one name in the
heading and another in the body, list both and give both the
same field name; from then on they always match.

Every place each piece of wording appears is replaced. Longer
wording is handled first, so "Acme Widgets, Inc." is replaced
before "Acme" would be. The report says how many times each
was found; "0" usually means the spelling or punctuation
differs from the contract.

Each row turns one piece of wording into one field. For
plural wording, type the format yourself in the Format box,
such as  plural: work, works.  Anything more involved, like
"10 years" becoming {{Term}} {{Term | plural: year, years}},
is quickest done afterwards in Word.

Contracts often arrive as an earlier agreement edited with
Track Changes on, and with reviewers' comments. With the tick
box "Accept all tracked changes and remove comments first"
(on unless you untick it), the template starts from the
wording as finally changed, exactly as if you had chosen
Accept All Changes in Word, and none of the old redlines or
comments reach a contract. The report says how many there
were.

The new template has Track Changes switched off, so the
editing you do next is not recorded as revisions. With the
tick box "Reset letter spacing to normal" (on unless you
untick it), it also loses squeezed or stretched letter
spacing. Contracts converted from PDF, or with lines fitted
by hand, are full of it, and it leaves odd gaps once a
different name changes the length of a line.

The original contract is not changed. Open the new template in
Word to add the {{#if}} markers around optional clauses, then
put it in the templates folder.


---------------------------------------------------------------
 WHEN SOMETHING LOOKS WRONG
---------------------------------------------------------------

Read the report panel first.

A {{field}} came through unchanged
  The braces were typed with something between them that is
  not a plain brace, or one is missing. Check this template
  lists it as a problem.

"is open in Word"
  Word locks files it has open. Close the contract, or the
  template, and try again.

Clause numbers did not renumber
  They were typed by hand. Use Word's automatic numbering.

The wrong state's clause appeared
  The {{#if}} compares exact wording (ignoring capitals).
  "Texas" and "TX" are different. A choice field avoids this.


---------------------------------------------------------------
 WHICH BUILD
---------------------------------------------------------------

The build number is in the title bar and in Help > About.
Settings (the templates folder, the last template, the field
set chosen for each template, Party 1 as last used, where
contracts were last saved, the two tick boxes) are kept in a
small file in your home folder, .tmai-contract-builder.json.
Deleting it simply resets them.
